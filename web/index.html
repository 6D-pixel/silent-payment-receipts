What this solves

For shops: one address, forever

Dana shows one silent-payment address for every order. She never makes or tracks new addresses, and each payment still lands in a fresh output that nobody can link to her or to her other payments.

For buyers: proof you paid

Silent payments leave the payer nothing to point to. A receipt proves “I paid this shop, this amount, for this order”. Anyone can check it against the chain, without your private keys and without the shop's help.

For marketplaces: settle “never paid”

The marketplace checks the receipt against the chain and rules. It holds no money and no keys, and counts each payment for one order only.

What's new

Silent payments (BIP-352) already give a shop one private, reusable address. What was missing is proof of payment. A receipt is a 33-byte shared secret and a 64-byte proof (BIP-374 DLEQ), signed over the transaction, the shop's address and the order. The usual workaround, sharing the output tweak, can be faked with a hidden way to take the money back; a receipt can't. As far as we know, no wallet or payment server offers receipts for silent payments today.

  1. The shop sends you an invoice

    Dana's Phone Shop shows the same address for every order. The invoice adds the order number, and your receipt will name it.

  2. You pay it

    Your wallet sends the amount to the invoice address. A block is mined straight away, so the payment is confirmed.

  3. What everyone else sees

    Anyone can read this transaction. Nothing in it says who was paid: the shop's output is a fresh, random-looking key.

  4. You make a receipt

    Your wallet proves that the coins it spent paid this address, for this order only. The proof is 97 bytes and contains no private keys.

  5. The marketplace checks it

    The marketplace fetches the transaction from the chain itself and checks the receipt against it. It trusts nothing else in the file.

  6. Now try to cheat

    Each attempt goes through the same check the marketplace just ran.

    Use it for another order

    Change the order in your receipt to Order #2002 and claim this payment again.

    Edit the proof

    Change one character of the 64-byte proof.

    Pay with a hidden way back

    Pay 0.10 BTC to a key built so the usual "share the tweak" check passes, while you keep a way to take the money back.
