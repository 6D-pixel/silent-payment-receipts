Pay privately.
Still prove it.

Bitcoin silent payments hide who got paid. That protects privacy, but leaves the payer with nothing to show if the shop says the money never came. Here you pay a shop, then prove the payment to a third party without sharing any private keys.

Simulated chain

Real, signed Bitcoin transactions and real proofs, run by the project's Python code in your browser. What's simulated: no network and no mining. A block is a counter, mined right after each payment.

Loading Python into your browser…

  1. The shop sends you an invoice

    Dana's Phone Shop gives every order its own payment address, so a payment to it can only be for that order.

  2. You pay it

    Your wallet sends the amount to the invoice address. A block is mined straight away, so the payment is confirmed.

  3. What everyone else sees

    Anyone can read this transaction. Nothing in it says who was paid: the shop's output is a fresh, random-looking key.

  4. You make a receipt

    Your wallet proves that the coins it spent paid this address, for this order only. The proof is 97 bytes and contains no private keys.

  5. A marketplace checks it

    The marketplace fetches the transaction from the chain itself and checks the receipt against it. It trusts nothing else in the file.

  6. Now try to cheat

    Each attempt goes through the same check the marketplace just ran.

    Use it for another order

    Change the order in your receipt to Order #2002 and claim this payment again.

    Edit the proof

    Change one character of the 64-byte proof.

    Pay with a hidden way back

    Pay 0.10 BTC to a key built so the usual "share the tweak" check passes, while you keep a way to take the money back.
